Effects of war risk on financial markets

News intensity, event clustering and cross-asset responses to conflict-related information.

The research question

How do changes in the intensity of news about an ongoing conflict relate to financial-market movements?

The study adapts the Rigobon–Sack heteroskedasticity approach and supplements it with signed local projections, using news about the 2026 Iran conflict to investigate relationships across equities, Treasuries, credit, oil, gold and the dollar.

Collecting and clustering news

GDELT's Global Knowledge Graph was queried through BigQuery for a February 28–September 22, 2026 sample. Collection targeted ten financial, international and regional outlets using Iran- and conflict-related search terms. Observed timestamps were converted to Eastern Time and mapped to market dates.

After URL and within-outlet headline deduplication, TF–IDF unigram and bigram vectors represented articles. A time-blocked cosine-similarity threshold of 0.52 within a 48-hour window linked potentially related articles through union-find clustering, avoiding a dense all-pairs similarity matrix.

Constructing high- and low-news samples

The daily intensity score combines robustly standardized article volume, publication breadth, independent source-group breadth, event breadth and a market-relevance component.

Match high and low news days

High-news dates fall at or above the 75th percentile, with coverage from at least three independent source groups and a financial publication. Low-news controls fall within the bottom half of scores and exclude identified major confounders. Minimum-cost matching selects unique controls close in trading-day time.

The matched dates create comparably timed pooled samples; the estimator uses the difference between their second-moment matrices, not within-pair differences.

Estimating market responses

Identification through heteroskedasticity

The difference in market covariance matrices across high- and low-news regimes is used to isolate a common factor, with the two-year Treasury yield serving as the identifying anchor.

ΔΩ = ΩH − ΩL

The variance change in the anchor was modest, making the estimates imprecise. A positive one-standard-deviation yield shock is the more relevant comparison for this episode.

Signed local projections

High-news days were classified as escalation, de-escalation or mixed events. Local projections relate cumulative asset changes at horizons of zero, one, two, three and five trading days to directional news-intensity shocks. Controls include lagged outcomes, weekday effects and major scheduled macroeconomic announcements; Newey–West standard errors account for overlapping outcomes.

What moved, and when?

Same-day escalation effects are generally small and statistically insignificant. The stronger associations emerge over subsequent trading days.

The increase in Treasury yields differs from the flight-to-safety response documented in the earlier Iraq war study. Gold also moves contrary to conventional safe-haven expectations in this specification. Oil and credit spreads move in expected directions, but their estimates are imprecise.

Interpretation. These estimates are conditional associations per modeled news-intensity shock. They are not predictions of guaranteed market movements or fully identified causal effects.

Conclusions and limitations

The strongest evidence is for delayed cross-asset relationships rather than immediate, uniform market responses to war news.

News may reflect market reactions as well as events, and information may be anticipated. GDELT timestamps are observation timestamps rather than verified original publication times. The weak variance difference in the identifying yield anchor further limits the covariance estimator. The directional local projections provide complementary evidence but do not remove these identification challenges.
